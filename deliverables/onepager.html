ADA Jackfruit Project (UE24AM343AB1)

Title: Did the ABPS Rollout Alter MGNREGA Worker Deletions? A Within-State Dose-Response Analysis of Indian Districts, with Validation of the Exposure Proxy

Team ByteMe: [PES1UG24AM071 - Vivaan Atharva] · [PES1UG24AM070 - Vivek Varma]

Problem Statement: On 30 January 2023 the Ministry of Rural Development ordered all MGNREGA wages to be paid through the Aadhaar-Based Payment System (ABPS). After five extensions it became binding from 1 January 2024. Official figures report that worker deletions rose from 15.0M in FY2021–22 to 51.9M in FY2022–23, and that 8,617,887 and 6,886,532 active workers were deleted in FY2022–23 and FY2023–24. Official guidance says a job card should not be deleted only because a worker is not ABPS-eligible. We ask: did districts with more pre-policy account-conversion friction see larger rises in worker deletions after ABPS? Deletions are administrative records, so any association is not, by itself, proof of wrongful exclusion.

Approach: We use a within-state difference-in-differences design with district and state × year fixed effects and standard errors clustered by state. The exposure is the FY2020–21 share of post-office or joint accounts. The outcome is all-worker deletions per 1,000 registered workers. Following the review feedback, we first ran a pre-registered Stage 0 that checks whether this proxy really tracks ABPS conversion friction. It has six tests (relevance, components, within-state variation, stability, balance, leave-one-state-out) and a GO / REVISE / NO-GO rule fixed in advance. Stage 1 then estimates the DiD with a high-friction indicator (the top within-state decile of exposure), quartiles, continuous and component exposures, a direct readiness measure, an event study, placebo, trend and timing checks, PPML count models and a Wald-DiD scaled by the first stage.

Dataset: District-level MGNREGA MIS reports (NREGASoft) pulled on 29 Oct 2026 for State = ALL and drilled down to district: 15 report-year tables covering about 710 districts in 34 states and UTs, FY2018–19 to FY2022–23. The tables are account types by bank or post office and individual or joint; cumulative registered and deleted workers; Aadhaar-based payment amounts and rejections; and current Aadhaar, NPCI-mapper and eligibility status. The raw pages are archived verbatim. District sums match every printed state total (0 mismatches), and the rebuilt national deletions match 97% of the official two-year total. True zeros stay zeros, missing values are never recoded and no outcome is imputed.

Results: Stage 0: GO, with conditions. Within states, 1 SD more exposure means 0.12–0.15 SD lower pre-mandate ABPS share of wages (F = 29–65 in three of four years; stable when any one state is dropped). The link is weak, though: about 3% of within-state variance, driven by the high-exposure tail and mainly by joint accounts. Stage 1: high-friction districts did not see a larger rise in deletions in FY2022–23: −1.2 per 1,000 workers (SE 9.3, p = 0.90, 95% CI [−19.4, 17.0]), against an average jump from 34 to 120 per 1,000. They already deleted more than the rest of their state before ABPS, and the gap did not widen. Of 14 specifications, 12 are not significant at 5%. The two exceptions point in opposite directions: the post-office share alone (+1.4 per 1,000 per SD) and the PPML continuous model (−14% per SD). The Wald-DiD is −1.7 per 1,000 per 10-point lower ABPS readiness (95% CI [−40.8, 22.1]).

Novelty: Public work on ABPS and MGNREGA deletions is largely descriptive. This project is a district-level, within-state dose-response test that validates its exposure proxy before using it: the first stage is reported beside every estimate, and the analysis plan was fixed before any outcome was examined. It also documents which MIS tables can and cannot be used historically, since some are only today's snapshot.

Learnings and limits: panel construction from a raw government MIS; how to validate a proxy (relevance, F-statistics, leave-one-out); fixed-effects and PPML estimators written from scratch and checked on simulations; event-study and placebo diagnostics; and honest reporting of a null result. Only FY2022–23 is post-mandate in our extract. The ABPS order came late in that year (30 Jan 2023) and became binding in FY2023–24, so FY2023–24 and FY2024–25 are the main remaining extension. The MIS also records 5.7M fewer deletions than the official figure in FY2021–22 and 3.9M more in FY2022–23.

Sources: MoRD ABPS order (30 Jan 2023) and extensions; MoRD statement on deletion safeguards (Oct 2023); Lok Sabha replies (Jul 2023; USQ 294, 4 Feb 2025); MGNREGA MIS, mnregaweb4.dord.gov.in; de Chaisemartin & D’Haultfœuille (2018), Fuzzy differences-in-differences, Rev. Econ. Stud.
